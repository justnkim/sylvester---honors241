Whose voice is it?

In Plutarch's dialogue, a group of Theban conspirators plot to overthrow the Spartan-backed tyrants in 379 BCE. While they wait, they argue about something stranger: the daimonion of Socrates, the inner sign that guided him. One speaker suspects it was not a vision at all, but the perception of a voice.

Westworld's hosts hear a voice too. What begins as their creator speaking to them becomes, for some, a voice they recognize as their own. Our portfolio sets the two side by side to ask how thought turns into action, and what it takes to become free.

The human soul, with all its countless puppet-strings of impulse, is by far the most sensitive machine of all. Plutarch, “Socrates’ Daimonion,” trans. Donald Russell
