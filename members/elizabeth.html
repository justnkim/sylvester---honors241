Build 1: Exploration of Timarchus' Myth

Plutarch’s writing of Timarchus’ myth indicates the elements of consciousness are the ‘soul’ which is inside of our bodies and the ‘daimon’ which is outside.The myth also affirms that the total consciousness that is formed is affected by the environment around it and due to this, is experienced differently from person-to-person. Plutarch presents these thoughts of consciousness that come to Timarchus as a vision, due to this, the ideas expressed are not intended to be interpreted as fact but more so as an exploration of one possibility of how consciousness works: a two part system of internal and external elements.

Firstly, even though the soul is presented as different from the body, they are not entirely separate. Plutarch presents the idea that every soul possesses intellect but becomes irrational once they merge with the body (108). I initially interpreted the ‘intellect’ suggested as the human brain, but I now believe Plutarch was referring to ‘daimon’- the aspect of consciousness that is left untouched by human desire. In contrast to this, the soul is the part of consciousness that is malleable and changed by human nature. With the idea that the body changes the soul, it is implied that we do not have full control of our actions which suggests that there is a subconscious that also shapes how we act. With an understanding of what Plutarch refers to as the soul, we can discuss the external part of consciousness.

In cases where the soul is not fully engulfed, Plutarch describes that the “purest element” floats outside the body (109). Plutarch continues by writing that the floating part is often referred to as intellect but it is actually, what he labels, daimon (109). Describing this aspect of thought in such a light provides the concept that our most thoughtful ideas do not come from our own brain but an intangible outside source that is connected to our soul. Outlining a divide between the thoughts from outside our bodies and the actions of our bodies provides a narrative that consciousness is not one complete, cohesive unit, but instead a multi-part system that does not always agree with itself. Describing consciousness as this system allows for a more complex understanding of how we think and act: some actions come from the neural pathways in our brain subconsciously, some from the soul within us, and others from our daimon outside of us. This leaves us with the question: how is the consciousness we individually experience formed to become unique to us? To answer this question I will examine the latter section of the passage where Plutarch touches on this subject.

Within Timarchus’ myth he sees the daimons flying with varying degrees of order, the voice explains to him that “[daimons] who exhibited a straight, orderly movement had souls made responsive to the rein by good breeding and education” (109). Stating this explains that when in the proper environment the “irrational” element of souls can be controlled by the intelligent daimons much easier. However, Plutarch continues by explaining that those who are not provided with a suitable environment each have a daimon that is fighting against an uncontrolled and emotional soul (109). This depiction allows for the interpretation that everyone has a daimon trying to guide them down the right path. I further interpreted this section to mean that those who fully rely on the insights from their soul may not even be aware of their daimon that is working to redirect them. People with an “uncontrolled soul” may experience guidance through ways that differ from intentional thought; therefore, Plutarch’s inclusion of environmental impacts on development provide an explanation as to why we experience consciousness differently from each other.

Overall, Timarchus’myth provides the idea that consciousness is a system made up of internal and external parts that are altered by our human experiences. Build 1

Through these aspects of Plutarch’s writing we get a view of how our consciousness could function that differs from other common conclusions about consciousness.
